# Checkout

Checkout is the path a buyer takes from a filled basket to a confirmed order. It is the single
most valuable flow on Acme Market: every point of conversion we lose here is lost GMV for our
sellers. This document states what checkout must do. How it is built is described in RFC-010,
which models checkout as a saga over the event bus, and the money-handling decisions are recorded
in ADR-005 (idempotency keys) and ADR-007 (asynchronous capture). Mobile web and the native apps
share the same requirements.

## Goals {#goals}

The checkout team commits to three goals for the next four quarters.

- Raise mobile checkout conversion from 2.4 percent to 3.0 percent, on the path to the 3.5
  percent three-year goal in the vision.
- Bring checkout p99 latency, measured from the buyer pressing "Place order" to seeing the
  confirmation page, under 1.5 seconds. Today it is 4.2 seconds, mostly because payment capture
  happens inside the request.
- Reduce the order defect rate caused by checkout, mainly duplicate charges and orders created
  without a successful payment, to fewer than 2 per 100,000 orders.

Non-goals for this period: guest checkout without an account, multi-currency baskets that mix
sellers in different currencies, and buy-now-pay-later.

## User stories {#user-stories}

- As a buyer, I can see the full price, including shipping and tax, before I commit, so that
  nothing surprises me on the card statement.
- As a buyer, I can buy from several sellers in one basket and pay once, while each seller gets
  their own order and ships independently.
- As a buyer on a flaky mobile connection, I can press "Place order" again after a timeout
  without being charged twice.
- As a buyer, I get a confirmation within a couple of seconds, even if the final charge
  completes a little later.
- As a seller, I only see an order once the buyer's payment is authorised, so I never start
  making something that will not be paid for.
- As a support agent, I can see every step a checkout went through, with timestamps, to answer
  "was I charged?" without asking engineering.

## Basket flow {#basket-flow}

The basket holds items from any number of sellers. Each line keeps the listing id, the variant,
the quantity and the price shown when the item was added. When the buyer opens checkout we
re-price every line; if a price changed or an item sold out, the buyer sees a clear notice and
must confirm the new total before continuing. We never silently change what the buyer pays.

Items in a basket are not reserved. Inventory is reserved only when the buyer places the order,
and the reservation lasts 15 minutes, which is long enough for 3-D Secure challenges and slow
banks. Baskets persist for 30 days for signed-in buyers and are merged when a buyer signs in on a
second device, keeping the higher quantity for duplicate lines. A basket is split into one order
per seller at placement time; the split, shipping per seller and tax per line are computed by the
pricing service so that the web and apps cannot disagree.

## Payment methods {#payment-methods}

At launch of the new checkout we support cards (Visa, Mastercard, American Express), the two
major wallet buttons on mobile, and stored cards for returning buyers. Card details never touch
our servers: the client tokenises them with the payment processor, and we store only the token,
the brand, the last four digits and the expiry month.

Strong customer authentication is handled by the processor's 3-D Secure flow; if a challenge is
required, the saga waits for the result rather than failing. Bank transfer and local methods in
Europe are planned for the second half of the year and must fit the same authorise-then-capture
model. A payment method that cannot be authorised separately from capture is out of scope,
because the asynchronous capture design in ADR-007 depends on that separation. Gift cards are
treated as a payment method with their own balance ledger and can be combined with one card.

## Failure handling {#failure-handling}

The worst checkout failure is charging a buyer twice; the second worst is creating an order that
is never paid. Both are prevented by design rather than by after-the-fact clean-up.

Every "Place order" request carries an idempotency key generated by the client when the checkout
page loads, as [ADR-005](../../adr/adr-005-idempotency-keys.md) requires for every write API. If
the buyer retries after a timeout, or the app retries automatically, the server recognises the key
and returns the original result instead of starting a second checkout. The payment processor
receives the same key, so even a retry that reaches the processor cannot create a second charge.

If authorisation fails, the inventory reservation is released and the buyer sees the decline
reason in plain words. If capture fails after the order is confirmed, the saga retries with
backoff and, after the retry window, cancels the order and notifies buyer and seller. Orders are
never shown to sellers before authorisation succeeds. RFC-010 describes the compensation steps in
detail.

## Pricing {#pricing access=finance}

This section is confidential to finance. The platform take rate on each order is 6.5 percent of
the item subtotal plus 0.30 USD per order, charged to the seller and deducted before payout.
Shipping is excluded from the percentage; sales tax collected on behalf of sellers is excluded
entirely. Payment processing costs, which average 2.1 percent plus 0.25 USD on our card mix, are
absorbed by the platform, so the net margin per order is roughly 4.4 percent minus fixed costs.

A planned increase to 7.2 percent, codenamed Tern, is modelled for the second quarter of 2027.
Finance estimates it adds 11 million USD of annual revenue at current GMV with an expected seller
churn of under 2 percent if paired with the faster payout schedule in PRD-004. Tern requires 60
days of written notice to sellers under the vision's non-goals, so the announcement must go out
no later than early February. Until then the number must not appear in seller-facing copy.

## Metrics {#metrics}

We judge checkout by a small number of measures, reported weekly on the checkout dashboard.

- Checkout conversion: orders placed divided by checkout pages opened, split by platform.
- Payment authorisation rate: successful authorisations divided by attempts, split by card brand
  and issuing country; a drop of more than one point in an hour pages the on-call engineer.
- Placement latency: p50, p95 and p99 from "Place order" to the confirmation page.
- Duplicate charge rate: charges refunded as duplicates per 100,000 orders. The target is zero
  and anything above 2 is an incident.
- Orphan orders: orders confirmed whose capture finally failed, per 100,000 orders.
- Basket abandonment at the price-change notice, to check that re-pricing is not driving buyers
  away.

## Open questions {#open-questions}

- Should we allow a buyer to edit the basket after placing the order but before the seller
  accepts it? Sellers like flexibility; finance worries about partial captures and refunds.
- How long should an authorised but not yet captured payment wait before we cancel it? Card
  networks allow up to seven days for most cards, but holding funds that long harms buyers with
  low limits.
- Do we show the seller's estimated dispatch date at checkout? It improves trust but some sellers
  under-promise, which might lower conversion.
- Should stored cards be offered by default or behind a toggle, given the privacy concerns raised
  in the last buyer survey?

Decisions on these questions will be recorded as ADRs and linked from this document.
